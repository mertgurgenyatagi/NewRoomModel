# Style Analysis

Foundation for the design of a ~13 m² studio apartment, based on the 12 image files in [style_refs/](style_refs/). Two of them are the same picture (`Studio apartment ideas-board-image-1` and `-image-3`, the rainy-evening bookshelf divider), so there are 11 distinct references. Counts below are approximate.

## 1. Style in one line

**Warm, plant-filled, book-heavy "cozy naturalist" studio**: honey-toned wood, layered natural textiles, lush greenery and low, warm light, with a shelf unit that divides sleeping from living/working.

Closest labels: warm Scandi-boho, "cottagecore-meets-urban-studio", dark-academia-lite (the books, the vintage rugs), with a mid-century furniture accent.

## 2. What repeats across the references

| Element | Frequency | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Wooden bookshelf, tall and open | 9 of 11 | The single strongest signal. Often used as a room divider. |
| Trailing / hanging plants (pothos, ivy) | 10 of 11 | Draped from the top of shelves, hung in windows, on wall shelves. |
| Large-leaf plants (monstera, rubber plant) | 8 of 11 | Floor pots in woven baskets, next to the window. |
| Honey/medium-brown wood floor | 11 of 11 | Warm, slightly orange; herringbone in one image, planks elsewhere. |
| Big window with sheer/linen curtain | 9 of 11 | Light is the main "decor" element. |
| Woven / rattan / jute | 9 of 11 | Rugs, pendant lamps, chair, baskets, side tables. |
| Layered, rumpled bedding | 9 of 11 | Linen or knit, never crisp hotel-style. |
| Warm point-source lighting | 11 of 11 | Pendants, lamps, fairy lights, candles. |
| Desk placed at a window | 6 of 11 | Work zone is tied to daylight, not tucked away. |

## 3. Colour palette

**Dominant (about 70%)**
- Warm honey oak: floors, shelves, desk, furniture (approx `#B9793F` to `#C98A4B`)
- Cream / warm off-white: walls, bedding, curtains (approx `#EFE4D0` to `#F5EEDD`)
- Natural fibre beige: jute rugs, linen, baskets (approx `#C9B18A`)

**Secondary (about 20%)**
- Plant green, leafy and saturated (approx `#4F7A3C` to `#2F5D2A`)
- Muted sage / olive green as a *textile and furniture* colour: bedding, throws, poufs, sofa, painted shelf (approx `#8FA482` sage, `#5C6B45` olive). Appears in 4 images and reads as the most cohesive "designed" choice.

**Accents (about 10%, use sparingly)**
- Terracotta / burnt orange: sofa, cushions, plant pots (approx `#B8542F`)
- Mustard / ochre: one statement chair or cushion (approx `#D19A1F`)
- Black: only in small hits (lamp, desk chair, book spines). Keeps things grounded.

**Avoid:** cool greys, pure white, chrome, high-gloss colour. The one image with a white desk and black mesh chair (new_refs 13) is the weakest fit; treat it as a "what not to do" for the work corner.

## 4. Materials and textures

- **Wood**: natural, visible grain, satin or matte finish. Light-to-mid tone, warm. Legs are slightly tapered (mid-century), shelf uprights are chunky and simple.
- **Textiles**: linen, chunky knit throws, boucle / textured cushions, corduroy or velvet on one hero seat. Everything is slightly wrinkled and soft.
- **Rugs**: two families. (a) Natural jute / braided round or rectangular rugs. (b) Vintage Persian / kilim in faded reds and blues, used when the room leans "collector". Pick one family for our room; mixing both only works at larger scale.
- **Woven**: rattan chair and side table, wicker pendant, seagrass baskets as plant covers and storage.
- **Ceramics and glass**: terracotta pots, small vases, mugs. Handmade look.
- **Walls**: plain warm white, lightly textured. Art is framed botanical prints and small gallery walls, kept in wood or black thin frames.

## 5. Light

This is what makes the references feel the way they do.

- **Daylight**: low golden-hour sun through sheer curtains. Curtains are always light, floaty, and never block the window.
- **Evening**: lots of small warm sources instead of one ceiling light. Around 2200 to 2700 K. Types seen:
  - Woven or rattan pendant (5 images)
  - White globe pendant (1 image)
  - Arc floor lamp (1 image)
  - Small mushroom / bedside table lamps (3 images)
  - Fairy lights along a window or shelf (3 images)
  - Candle on the coffee table
- **Rule of thumb**: 3 to 5 warm sources at different heights, none of them bright.

## 6. Layout ideas that fit a 13 m² studio

These are the spatial moves the references use and that work at our size.

1. **Open shelf as divider (strongest idea).** A tall, open, 2-sided bookcase perpendicular to the wall separates the bed from the desk/seating without blocking light. Seen in 5 references (Studio ideas 1/3/5, new_refs 2, 3). Open backs let the window light through.
2. **Bed along the window wall or in the corner**, with the shelf unit at its foot or side.
3. **Desk under or beside the window**, in the daylight. Either a built-in style plank desk on chunky legs (Studio ideas 2) or a desk integrated into the bottom of the shelf unit (new_refs 3).
4. **One "living" cluster only**: a small loveseat or one armchair plus a low coffee table on a rug. In 13 m² pick *either* sofa *or* armchair, not both.
5. **Floating wall shelves** for plants and books over the desk / bed head, so the floor stays free.
6. **Round or small rug** to zone the seating area; keeps circulation clear.
7. **Vertical use of space**: plants hanging from the ceiling or shelf tops, art stacked on the wall, tall bookcase to near ceiling height.

## 7. Furniture language

- **Bed**: low wooden frame or platform, plain headboard, no storage bulk visible. Bedding in cream / linen / sage.
- **Shelf unit**: solid wood or sage-painted wood, open, ~5 shelves, mixed height of books plus plants plus small objects. Clutter is curated, not empty.
- **Desk**: plain wood plank top, chunky or simple tapered legs. No glossy or white desks.
- **Chair**: light rattan / wicker or a simple wood spindle chair. One hero chair in mustard or terracotta is allowed.
- **Coffee table**: low, rustic or mid-century wood, with visible legs (keeps floor visible, which reads larger).
- **Side tables / stools**: rattan or small wooden stool, doubling as plant stands.

## 8. Decor and "life" details

- Books everywhere, some stacked horizontally on top of others (not perfectly tidy).
- Mug, candle, open book on the coffee table.
- Framed botanical / abstract prints, small and in groups of 3 to 6.
- A few sculptural objects: wooden figurines, vases, a record player.
- Plants are the main decor: aim for a mix of trailing (pothos), leafy floor (monstera), and small windowsill pots. Cover plastic pots with baskets or terracotta.

## 9. Two sub-moods present in the references

Our final design should choose a lean, but they share the same base.

| Mood | References | Traits |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Sunlit botanical (bright, airy)** | Studio ideas 2, 5; new_refs 3, 4, 11, 13 | Cream walls, jute rug, linen, lots of daylight, pale-to-honey wood, green plants. |
| **B. Evening-cozy (warm, moody)** | Studio ideas 1/3; new_refs 2, 8, 10, and the outlier | Amber lighting, olive/terracotta textiles, darker wood, vintage rugs. |

**Recommendation:** base the room on mood A for the materials and layout, and add mood B's lighting (many warm lamps, fairy lights) and its sage/terracotta accents. This gives a room that looks good in the day and at night, which matters for a small space you live in fully.

## 10. Outlier to treat carefully

`Studio apartment ideas-board-image-5` (posters, tapestry, red-orange lighting, Persian rugs, floor-level seating) is a *maximalist retro* look and is the least consistent with the rest. Useful takeaways only: layered vintage rugs and a warm-lit gallery wall of art. Do not adopt the saturated orange lighting or the floor-cushion sofa.

## 11. Design rules to carry forward

1. Wood + cream + green is the base; sage and terracotta are the only allowed accent colours, plus one mustard piece at most.
2. Every material must be natural or look natural (wood, linen, wool, jute, rattan, ceramic).
3. Light comes from many low warm sources; no single bright ceiling light.
4. Keep the window unobstructed: sheer curtains, low furniture in front of it, shelves with open backs.
5. Use an open shelf unit as the zoning device rather than walls or curtains.
6. Furniture is low or has visible legs, so the floor reads as continuous.
7. Plants at three levels: hanging, floor and shelf/windowsill.
8. Soft, slightly rumpled textiles; nothing stiff or shiny.
9. Curated clutter: books and objects are displayed, but each zone has one calm, empty surface.
10. Keep a clear walking path of about 60 to 70 cm through the room.

## 12. Open questions for the next step

Answers here will shape the Blender model.

- Room shape and dimensions (13 m² could be 3 × 4.3 m or 2.6 × 5 m), and where the window(s), door, and any radiator are.
- Do you need a kitchenette or is it separate?
- Bed size (single, 140 or 160 cm)?
- Do you want a real desk/work area, or a laptop-on-a-shelf setup?
- Preferred lean: sunlit botanical (A), evening-cozy (B), or the blended recommendation above?
- Sofa, armchair, or neither?
